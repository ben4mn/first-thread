# A first workshop

Use a real example small enough that someone can walk through the last time it happened. The fictional demo illustrates the controls; it is not evidence about a customer.

## 1. Agree on the boundaries · 5 minutes

Create a scenario. Name who or what it concerns, one event that starts it, and one intended outcome. Ask how the participant would recognize a successful result.

If several outcomes emerge, choose one for now and note the others. A greenfield scenario is valid: today may have no reliable route at all.

## 2. Walk the route · 15 minutes

Ask: “What happened next?” Add a step for each meaningful action or handoff. Capture the tool or team, the owner, the information received, and the information passed onward.

Select a step to open its detailed view. Record its surface and channel if those distinctions are useful. Ask what else touches it: another team, a data set, a customer experience, or an exception route.

Mark friction where it occurs. Keep unknowns blank instead of making the map look complete. Set each step to Assumption, Reported, or Observed and record why. These labels describe the workshop's evidence; the app does not verify them.

## 3. Define B and a first move · 10 minutes

Open **A better state**. Describe the customer or operator experience you want. Choose one manageable change and say why you expect it to help.

Name the evidence of success, the person responsible, and a fallback. A baseline may need to be measured before a target is defensible. Keep this as a proposal until the people responsible for the operation agree.

## 4. Hand it back · 5 minutes

Read the map back to the person doing the work. Ask which detail is wrong, where context disappears, and what is missing. Export the brief for discussion. Export the workspace so another person can continue editing.

Then visit **Company notebook**. Keep one statement, rewrite another, and choose one commercial assumption to test next.

## What to notice while using the prototype

- Can you agree on the scenario boundary without a long explanation?
- Does the route match a real example, including the awkward handoffs?
- Does selecting a step provide the useful detail missing from the overview?
- Which vocabulary feels unnatural or overlaps?
- What would the next participant need to do this without Ben or Paul explaining it?
- What feature did you actually miss while completing this workshop?

Bring those observations back to the repository. Work on one demonstrated gap at a time.
